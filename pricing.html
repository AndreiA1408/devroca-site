No price list. A quote for your project.

Nothing here is sold off a shelf, so nothing here has a shelf price. We work out what your project actually needs, talk it through, and you get one number in writing before any work starts.

HOW PRICING WORKS

Scope first, then a number.

A figure quoted before anyone knows what's being built is a guess, and a guess is what later turns into a revised invoice. So the scope comes first, and the price comes out of it.

You describe it

What the business does, what the site or app has to do, and any deadline you're working to. A rough description is enough to start.

We scope it together

A short conversation turns that into a specific list: which of the 15 services are in, how many pages, which functionality, which integrations.

You get one number

One total for that scope, in writing, with the list of what it covers. Priced on what you actually need, not on an hourly estimate that drifts.

Work starts

That number holds for the agreed scope. If you later want something added, it's scoped and quoted on its own before it's built.

WHAT MOVES THE NUMBER

What the quote is based on.

Three things decide what a project costs. All three are settled in the scoping conversation, before anything is quoted.

Which services are included

A small brochure site is a different job from a web app with logins, a booking system, and a second language. The total is assembled from the services you actually pick.

  • Priced per service, not per bundle
  • Nothing included that you didn't ask for

How much of each

Page count, number of languages, how much custom functionality sits behind the front end. This is the part that decides whether a service is a small job or a large one.

  • Scope stated in numbers, not adjectives
  • Written down before the price is quoted

One-off vs. ongoing

Build work is quoted once, for the agreed scope. Maintenance, monitoring, and content updates are quoted separately as monthly amounts, the same way.

  • Build and retainer priced separately
  • Monthly items billed monthly, no lock-in
WHAT YOU GET

The quote is a document.

Not a number in a chat message. Every quote has the same four parts, so you can read the whole thing before agreeing to any of it.

What the quote itself contains

Written before work starts, and specific enough that you can tell exactly what you are and aren't buying.

  1. The service line itemsWhich of the 15 services are in scope, named individually.
  2. The numbers behind themPage count, number of languages, which functionality — scope stated in figures, not adjectives.
  3. The quoted priceOne total for that scope, plus any monthly amounts listed separately.
  4. What isn't includedAnything discussed but left out, written down, so a later addition is an obvious separate quote.
